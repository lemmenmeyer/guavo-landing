About revenue-based financing

What is revenue-based financing?

Revenue-based financing is the purchase of a portion of your future revenue at a discount, rather than a loan against your assets. Guavo advances you a lump sum today, and you remit an agreed percentage of your weekly deposits until the agreed total is paid. Because remittance is tied to a percentage of what you actually collect, the amount moves with your sales instead of staying fixed regardless of how the month went.

How is this different from a bank loan?

Three differences matter most. First, it is a purchase of future receivables, not a loan, so there is no interest rate that compounds over time. Second, there is no collateral requirement and no lien on your property. Third, approval looks primarily at your revenue and bank activity rather than at a credit score, which is why businesses that a bank would decline on paper are often a fit here.

Banks are usually cheaper if you qualify and can wait. Guavo is built for the cases where you cannot.

What can I use the funding for?

Any legitimate business purpose. Inventory, payroll, equipment, expansion, marketing, covering a slow season, or consolidating more expensive financing you already carry. We ask what you intend to use it for on the application because it helps us structure the offer, not because we restrict the categories.

Qualifying and applying

What do I need to qualify?

We look at how long you have been operating, how consistent your monthly revenue is, and your recent bank activity. We do not require collateral, and we do not decline on credit score alone. The fastest way to find out where you stand is to apply, because the application uses a soft credit pull that does not affect your score.

Does applying affect my credit score?

No. The application uses a soft credit pull, which does not affect your credit score and is not visible to other lenders as an inquiry. You authorize that pull explicitly on the application form.

What do I need to apply?

Basic information about the business and its owner, a photo of your government-issued identification, and your four most recent business bank statements. Most applicants finish in under three minutes.

The bank statements are the part that actually determines your offer, so having them ready is the difference between an answer today and an answer next week.

How quickly can I get funded?

Funds are typically in your account within 24 hours of approval. The application itself takes a few minutes, and review time depends mostly on how quickly we receive your bank statements.

How much funding can I get?

Guavo funds from $10,000 to $100,000. The amount you are offered is based primarily on your monthly revenue and how consistent it is, since remittance is a percentage of what you collect.

Costs and payments

What does it cost?

Your offer states one fixed total repayment amount before you sign anything. There is no compounding interest, no rate that accrues over time, and no hidden fees. What the offer shows is what you remit in total.

The specific amount depends on your revenue, time in business, and the funding amount, so we quote it per deal rather than publishing a single figure. If anything in your offer is unclear, ask us before you sign and we will walk you through it line by line.

How do payments work?

You authorize a weekly debit from your business bank account. The weekly amount is calculated from an agreed percentage of your projected weekly receivables. Payments continue weekly until the agreed total has been remitted.

What happens if my sales drop?

You can request a reconciliation. We compare what we actually collected against the agreed percentage of your actual receivables for the period. If we collected more than that percentage, we refund the difference within five days of your request, or credit it against future remittances if you prefer. We also adjust your weekly amount going forward so it reflects what your business is really doing.

This is written into the agreement, not a courtesy we extend case by case.

Terms and protections

Do you use confessions of judgment?

No. The Guavo agreement does not include a confession of judgment. A confession of judgment is a clause that lets a funder obtain a judgment against you without going to court or notifying you first. They are common enough in this industry that it is worth asking any funder you speak with, and worth reading their answer carefully.

Is a personal guarantee required?

The owner signs a guarantee of performance, which is narrower than it sounds. It is not a guarantee that the business will succeed or that you will personally repay if sales fall short. It applies to specific breaches set out in the agreement, such as diverting receivables to another account, misrepresenting your finances, or cancelling the payment authorization while a balance is outstanding. Ordinary business underperformance does not trigger it.

What happens if my business fails?

Because this is a purchase of future receivables rather than a loan, you are not required to remit the remaining balance if your business genuinely stops generating revenue and you have not breached the agreement. That risk sits with us, and it is a real difference from a loan, where the debt survives the business.
